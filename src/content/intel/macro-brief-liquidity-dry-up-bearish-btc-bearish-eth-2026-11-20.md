The market is suffocating under a blanket of **Extreme Fear**, with the Fear & Greed Index hovering at single digits—**10** to **13**—for the entire past week. This is not a healthy correction; it is a liquidity vacuum. Bitcoin sits at **$61,759** and Ethereum at **$1,746.71**, both bleeding over **3%** in the last 24 hours as the Federal Reserve’s hawkish posture continues to drain risk premiums.

## The Setup

The catalyst for this dry spell is the **FOMC Meeting Minutes** released on **2026-11-18**. The document offered no dovish relief. Instead, it confirmed that policymakers remain deeply concerned about sticky inflation, specifically citing the **Core Retail Sales** print from **2026-11-17** where the forecast of **0.4%** month-over-month growth signaled underlying demand remains hotter than the Fed wants. When the **Core Retail Sales m/m** data came in slightly cooler than the previous **0.9%** but still above the **0.1%** soft-landing consensus, the market did not celebrate relief. It sold off. The logic is simple: if consumer spending is still robust, the Fed will not cut rates to save equities.

Chairman Warsh’s comments on **2026-11-16** reinforced this. He did not hint at a pivot. He hinted at patience. In a market already leveraged to a rate cut, patience is poison. The absence of any high-impact USD events for the rest of this week is not a reprieve; it is a trap. Traders are holding leveraged long positions into a void, waiting for a signal that isn't coming.

## BTC

Bitcoin is trading in a clear downward drift. The daily close below **$62,000** is significant. It marks the failure of buyers to defend the psychological support level that has held for months. The **3.35%** drop in 24 hours is not a panic sell-off; it is a slow bleed of liquidity.

Our bias is **Bearish**. The macro backdrop is hostile. With the Fed signaling no immediate rate cuts, the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets like BTC remains punishingly high. We expect BTC to test the **$60,000** level in the coming days. This is not a floor; it is a pivot point. If BTC breaks **$60,000** on high volume, the next target is **$58,500**, where the last significant on-chain accumulation layer sits.

The invalidation case is narrow. We would only reverse this bias if we see a sudden, unexplained spike in US Treasury yields or a hawkish surprise from a regional Fed president. Until then, shorts on rallies are the statistical edge. The trend is down, and the macro data supports it.

## ETH

Ethereum is following BTC, but with higher beta. A **3.28%** drop in 24 hours to **$1,746.71** shows that ETH is struggling to decouple from Bitcoin’s macro sensitivity. The ETH/BTC pair is weakening, indicating that capital is rotating out of altcoins and into stablecoins or back into dollars.

We are **Bearish** on ETH. The narrative of "ETH as a yield asset" is being tested by the high real yield environment. When you can get **5%** risk-free in short-term T-bills, paying gas fees and staking yields on ETH becomes less attractive. We expect ETH to test **$1,700** soon. This level has acted as support multiple times, but with liquidity drying up, previous support levels become resistance.

The key level to watch is **$1,700**. A daily close below this confirms the breakdown. The invalidation case requires a broad risk-on rally in equities, specifically a surge in Nasdaq futures, which is unlikely given the FOMC minutes.

## Calendar Risks

There are no high-impact USD events scheduled for the rest of this week. This is the primary risk. The market is trading on momentum and fear, not data. A sudden geopolitical shock or an unexpected tweet from a Fed official can flip the bias instantly. However, the absence of scheduled events means there is no catalyst to reverse the trend. The path of least resistance remains down.

We are watching the **Core PCE Price Index** for next week, but it is outside our current coverage window. For now, the market is blind. This blindness favors the short side.
