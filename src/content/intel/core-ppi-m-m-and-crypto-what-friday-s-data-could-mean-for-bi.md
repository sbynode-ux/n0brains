## What is Core PPI m/m?

The Producer Price Index (PPI) measures the average change over time in the selling prices received by domestic producers for their output. Think of it as an inflation gauge from the perspective of the producers. "Core PPI" excludes volatile food and energy prices, offering a clearer picture of underlying inflationary trends. "Month-over-month" (m/m) indicates the change from the previous month.

Essentially, Core PPI m/m tells us how much prices are changing at the producer level before they hit consumers. A higher-than-expected figure suggests that producers are facing increased costs, which they might pass on to consumers, potentially fueling inflation. Conversely, a lower-than-expected figure suggests cooling inflationary pressures.

## Why Does Core PPI Matter for Bitcoin and Crypto?

Central banks, most notably the U.S. Federal Reserve, closely monitor inflation data like PPI to inform their monetary policy decisions. Here's how it generally plays out:

*   **Higher-than-expected Core PPI:** This indicates persistent inflation. Central banks might interpret this as a signal to maintain a hawkish stance, potentially leading to higher interest rates or a slower pace of rate cuts. Higher interest rates typically strengthen the dollar and make riskier assets like Bitcoin less attractive, as investors seek safer, yield-bearing alternatives. This scenario could lead to downward pressure on Bitcoin and the broader crypto market.

*   **Lower-than-expected Core PPI:** This suggests inflation is cooling. This could give central banks more flexibility to adopt a dovish stance, potentially leading to interest rate cuts sooner or at a faster pace. Lower interest rates generally weaken the dollar and make riskier assets more appealing, as the cost of borrowing decreases and traditional returns become less attractive. This scenario could provide upward momentum for Bitcoin and the crypto market.
