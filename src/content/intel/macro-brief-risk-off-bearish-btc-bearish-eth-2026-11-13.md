The market is pricing in a soft landing that doesn't exist, and the only thing keeping **BTC** above **$69,000** is the hope that next week's inflation data will surprise to the upside.

It won't. The **Core CPI** released on November 10 held steady at the forecasted **0.2%** month-over-month, down from **0.3%**, while headline **CPI** slowed to **0.3%** from **0.6%**. On paper, this is disinflationary progress. In practice, it is a liquidity trap. The market is reacting not to the data, which was benign, but to the realization that the Federal Reserve has nowhere left to cut without igniting the very inflation it just tamed. We are in a stalemate. The upside is capped by macro constraints, and the downside is accelerated by the sheer exhaustion of leveraged long positions that have been held against all statistical probability.

## The Setup — what released and what didn't, why it matters

The **Core CPI** print was a non-event for direction but a massive event for conviction. The market expected a "relief rally." Instead, we saw immediate selling pressure. This divergence tells us that the narrative has shifted from "Fed pivot" to "Fed trapped." When the Fed is trapped, liquidity dries up, and volatility expands.

More importantly, the sentiment data confirms the structural weakness. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index has been stuck in **Extreme Fear** territory, hovering between **20** and **29** for the entire past week. This is not a healthy consolidation. This is capitulation disguised as stability. When sentiment stays this low for this long, it usually precedes a violent flush of leverage before any sustainable recovery can begin. The market is waiting for a catalyst, and that catalyst is this week's **Core PPI**.

## BTC — directional bias, bearish, key levels to watch, what invalidates the call

**BTC** is currently trading at **$69,281**, having dropped nearly **5%** in the last 24 hours. The bias is bearish. The daily trend is making lower lows, and the momentum indicators are flashing red. The key level to watch is **$68,500**. If **BTC** closes a daily candle below this level, it exposes the **$66,000** zone, which is the last major support block before a structural breakdown to **$62,000**.

The invalidation scenario is simple: **BTC** must reclaim and hold above **$71,500** with volume. Anything less is a bear trap. The current price action is consistent with a "bull trap" setup, where retail traders buy the dip into support, only to be liquidated when the next macro event hits. The leverage ratio on the long side is too high for this environment to sustain upward pressure without a significant liquidity injection.

## ETH — bearish, key levels to watch, what invalidates the call

**ETH** is underperforming **BTC**, trading at **$1,958.05**, down **3.07%** in 24 hours. The bias is bearish. The **ETH/BTC** pair is breaking down, which means we are not just dealing with a macro sell-off, but a rotation out of altcoins into stablecoins or **BTC** as a relative safe haven (which is still falling).

The key level is **$1,920**. A break below this triggers a retest of **$1,850**, the psychological floor. The invalidation scenario requires **ETH** to break above **$2,050** and hold. Given the current sentiment and the lack of positive catalysts in the DeFi sector, this is unlikely. The correlation between **ETH** and traditional tech equities is strengthening, meaning if the Nasdaq dips, **ETH** will likely drop harder.

## Calendar Risks — top 2-3 upcoming events that could flip the call

The only event that matters this week is the **Core PPI** and **PPI** data release on **November 13** at **13:30 UTC**. The forecast is **0.3%** for **Core PPI**, a significant slowdown from the previous **0.7%**. If the data comes in hot—above **0.4%**—it will confirm that inflation is sticky, and the "Fed trapped" narrative will accelerate, pushing **BTC** down sharply.

If the data comes in softer than **0.2%**, we might see a short squeeze, but it will likely be fleeting. The broader trend is down, and a pop in **PPI** data is unlikely to reverse the structural damage done by weeks of extreme fear. The risk is asymmetrical: the downside risk from a hot print is far greater than the upside potential from a cool print.

## Market Context

**BTC** is consolidating in a tight range with declining volume, indicating a lack of buyer interest. DeFi TVL has been stagnant, showing no inflow from new capital. Sentiment is at **Extreme Fear**, which is historically a contrarian buy signal, but only after the flush is complete. Until we see a volume spike and a break of local resistance, the path of least resistance is down.
