The market didn't crash on the jobs report. It collapsed because the report confirmed the Fed has more work to do. **Average Hourly Earnings** came in at **0.3%** month-over-month, beating the **0.1%** forecast and the **0.1%** previous print. This is the kind of wage stickiness that forces central banks to keep rates higher for longer. The result is not a bounce. It is a continuation of the liquidity squeeze.

## The Setup

The **Non-Farm Employment Change** came in at **65K**, a significant beat against the **29K** forecast and the previous **29K** print. On the surface, a strong jobs number is good for the economy. In the current macro regime, it is poison for risk assets. The **Unemployment Rate** held steady at **4.2%**, matching forecasts, which means the labor market is not cooling fast enough to trigger a rate cut narrative.

The market had priced in a soft landing. The data just priced in a "higher for longer" reality. When wages stay sticky, the Federal Reserve cannot pivot. When the Fed cannot pivot, liquidity remains tight. When liquidity is tight, leveraged crypto positions get hunted.

The **Congressional Elections** on **November 3** added a layer of political uncertainty, but the immediate price action is driven by macro data. The markets are digesting the fact that inflation is not dead; it is just waiting for a moment to breathe. That moment is now. The beat on wages and jobs tells the Fed they can afford to wait. For traders, it means the bid is weak.

## BTC — Directional Bias

**Bitcoin** is trading at **$75,920**, down **0.16%** in the last 24 hours. This lack of momentum is not a sign of stability. It is a sign of exhaustion. Buyers are stepping aside, waiting for a clearer macro signal that isn't coming.

The bias is **Bearish**. The conviction is high. The wage data reinforces the view that real yields will remain elevated, putting downward pressure on non-yielding assets like Bitcoin. The technical setup is fragile. Any bounce is likely to be sold into, especially if volume remains low.

Key levels to watch:
- **Support** at **$74,000**. A break below this opens the door to **$72,000**.
- **Resistance** at **$78,000**. A reclaim of this level requires a fundamental shift in rate expectations, which is not on the horizon.

Invalidation of the bearish call would require a surprise dovish signal from the Fed or a sharp drop in wage growth in the next report. Neither is likely. The path of least resistance is lower.

## ETH — Directional Bias

**Ethereum** is trading at **$2,243.14**, up a marginal **0.12%** in the last 24 hours. This outperformance relative to BTC is deceptive. It is driven by short-term technicals and ETF flows, not macro tailwinds. When risk-off sentiment deepens, ETH underperforms BTC on the way down due to its higher beta and lower liquidity depth in some venues.

The bias is **Bearish**. The conviction is moderate to high. Ethereum is not immune to the macro headwinds. If Bitcoin breaks **$74,000**, Ethereum will likely follow with a larger percentage drop. The correlation between BTC and ETH remains strong in risk-off environments.

Key levels to watch:
- **Support** at **$2,150**. A break below this confirms a deeper correction.
- **Resistance** at **$2,350**. Unlikely to hold without BTC support.

Invalidation of the bearish call for ETH would require a surge in DeFi activity or a major protocol upgrade announcement that shifts narrative focus away from macro. Neither is imminent. The macro tide is pulling ETH down with BTC.

## Calendar Risks

The current calendar week has no high-impact USD events scheduled. This is not a calm period; it is a holding pattern. The market is waiting for the next data dump. The risk here is not an event, but the absence of one. In the absence of positive catalysts, the default direction is lower.

The next major risk is the upcoming CPI release. Until then, traders should expect choppy, downward-biased action. The lack of events means liquidity is thin, making it easier for large players to push prices down into weak pockets of liquidity.
