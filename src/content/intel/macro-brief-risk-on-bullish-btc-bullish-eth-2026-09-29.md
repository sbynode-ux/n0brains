The Fear & Greed Index has sat comfortably in the **70–74** range for the last seven days, signaling a market that is confident, complacent, and positioned for upside.

There is no immediate macro fire to extinguish this rally. The past week saw zero high-impact USD releases, leaving the market to digest its own momentum. In the absence of negative news, crypto defaults to its baseline behavior: liquidity-driven appreciation. We are in a "no news is good news" regime, where the lack of volatility is itself a bullish signal for risk assets.

## The Setup

The absence of data is the data. For the past seven days, the economic calendar has been clean. No CPI. No PPI. No Fed speeches. This vacuum allowed the Fear & Greed Index to drift upward from **70** to **74**, a clear indication that traders are rotating from cash into beta.

When the macro feed is quiet, liquidity seeks yield. Spot Bitcoin ETF flows have been positive, and funding rates remain constructive. The market is not pricing in a recession; it is pricing in a continuation of the current cycle. This is not a time to hedge. This is a time to leverage.

The lack of high-impact prints means the market is free to move based on technicals and sentiment alone. Sentiment is our primary driver right now. When greed is this consistent, corrections are shallow and buying opportunities are fleeting. The setup favors the long side until proven otherwise by an external shock.

## BTC — directional bias, conviction, key levels to watch, what invalidates the call

Bitcoin is the anchor, and it is behaving exactly as we expect in a risk-on environment. The bias is **Bullish**.

The conviction is high because the path of least resistance is up. Without a macro headline to trigger a liquidity squeeze, BTC can ride the momentum of the Fear & Greed Index. We expect BTC to test higher liquidity pools, targeting the next major resistance zone where options expiry pain might create a temporary ceiling.

Key levels to watch are the recent swing highs. A break above these levels with volume confirms the continuation. A failure to break with a drop in volume suggests a pullback to re-accumulate.

What invalidates this call? A sudden spike in the DXY (US Dollar Index) or a negative surprise in the upcoming **Core PCE** data. If inflation comes in hotter than the **0.3%** forecast, the risk-on narrative breaks instantly. Until then, we buy the dips.

## ETH — same structure as BTC

Ethereum is outperforming BTC on sentiment, driven by its role in the broader DeFi ecosystem. The bias is **Bullish**.

ETH is sensitive to the same macro forces but amplifies them through its beta. As risk appetite increases, capital flows into yield-bearing assets like ETH. The **71–74** Greed index supports this rotation. We expect ETH/BTC to stabilize or rise as traders seek higher yields in the DeFi sector.

Key levels for ETH are its own resistance zones against BTC. A break above these confirms the outperformance. A drop below suggests a rotation back into pure store-of-value BTC.

Invalidation for ETH is similar to BTC but more sensitive. A drop in DeFi TVL or a regulatory headline targeting smart contracts would break this bias. However, with no such news in the pipeline, the path is clear. We are long ETH.

## Calendar Risks

The week ahead is packed with USD data, starting immediately. These events are the only things that can flip our bias from Bullish to Neutral or Bearish.

**Core PCE Price Index m/m** (2026-09-30T12:30) is the star of the show. The forecast is **0.3%** vs previous **0.2%**. A beat here (higher inflation) is bearish for crypto. A miss (lower inflation) is bullish. This single number will dictate the direction for the rest of the week.

**Non-Farm Employment Change** (2026-10-02T12:30) is the secondary risk. The forecast is **90K** vs previous **162K**. A significant miss (jobs cooling faster than expected) could trigger a recession fear trade, which is initially bearish for crypto but may later pivot to rate-cut hopes. We watch the unemployment rate closely; a rise above **4.1%** is a red flag.

**Average Hourly Earnings m/m** (2026-10-02T12:30) provides the wage-inflation link. Sticky wages keep the Fed hawkish. We expect this to be inline, but any surprise upward move will pressure risk assets.
