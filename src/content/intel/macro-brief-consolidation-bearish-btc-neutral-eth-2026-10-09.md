The **FOMC Meeting Minutes** released on Wednesday confirmed that policymakers are not in a rush to cut, yet the market’s immediate reaction was muted—a sign of exhaustion, not strength.

We are trading in a consolidation regime. The aggressive risk-on rally that pushed **Fear & Greed** to **73** earlier this week has stalled. Price action is rejecting higher levels, suggesting the easy money has been made. We are not here to predict the future with clairvoyance. We are here to map the probabilities. The data points to a bearish bias for Bitcoin and a neutral, range-bound stance for Ethereum as we head into a calendar void.

## The Setup

The **FOMC Meeting Minutes** for **October 7** provided the primary catalyst this week. The document offered no surprises. It reiterated the committee’s focus on persistent inflation risks and the need for caution. There was no dovish pivot, no hint of emergency easing. The market had priced in stability, and the minutes confirmed it.

What matters is what did not happen. There were no high-impact USD events scheduled for the current week ahead. The macro calendar is effectively empty. This silence is dangerous. In crypto, liquidity dries up when attention shifts to non-financial events or simply retreats for the weekend. Thin liquidity amplifies volatility. A small sell-off can trigger cascading liquidations if the order book is shallow. A small bid can pump prices artificially. We are entering a zone where technicals dominate fundamentals.

Sentiment remains in **Greed**, hovering between **59** and **73** over the last seven days. This is a divergence. Sentiment is hot, but price is cold. When sentiment leads price, a correction is usually the mechanism to bring them back into alignment. The crowd is buying the dip, but the dip keeps getting shallower. This is a trap.

## BTC — directional bias, conviction, key levels to watch, what invalidates the call

Bitcoin is trading at **$82,501**, down **0.23%** in the last 24 hours. The trend is flat to down. We are bearish on the near-term directional bias for BTC. The rejection of higher highs suggests that sellers are stepping in at these levels. The conviction is moderate. We are not calling for a crash, but we are calling for a drift lower or a tight range that favors short-side entries on bounces.

Key level to watch is **$80,000**. This is the psychological support and the recent consolidation floor. If BTC breaks below **$80,000** on volume, we expect a rapid move toward **$78,000**. This invalidates the bull case for the week. The bear case is invalidated if BTC reclaims and holds above **$85,000** with strong volume. Until then, we assume the path of least resistance is down or sideways.

Liquidity maps show a dense cluster of longs stacked below current price. This makes **$80,000** a likely liquidation target. We do not trade hope. We trade liquidity. The setup favors sellers until proven otherwise.

## ETH — same structure as BTC

Ethereum is trading at **$2,490.88**, down **2.33%** in the last 24 hours. The bias is neutral. ETH is underperforming BTC relative to its recent strength, but it is not crashing. It is consolidating. The **2.33%** drop is significant, but it is within normal volatility bands for a weekend approach.

We expect ETH to range between **$2,400** and **$2,600**. A break below **$2,400** opens the door to **$2,300**. A break above **$2,600** signals a resumption of the uptrend. Currently, there is no catalyst to drive a breakout. The lack of macro events means ETH will likely follow BTC’s lead, but with less volatility. We are neutral. We are waiting for a clear signal.

The correlation between ETH and BTC is high. If BTC breaks **$80,000**, ETH will likely follow. If BTC holds, ETH will likely hold. The divergence is small. We treat them as a single asset class for now.

## Calendar Risks

The week ahead has **no** scheduled high-impact USD events. This is the primary risk. The absence of data means price discovery is driven by technical flows and whale activity. A single large liquidation event can swing the market **5%** in either direction. We advise caution. Do not over-leverage into a void.

If a surprise event emerges—central bank commentary, geopolitical news, or a major exchange outage—the bias flips instantly. We do not predict surprises. We prepare for them. Hold cash. Wait for the dust to settle.
