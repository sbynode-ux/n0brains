The market is long because nobody is stopping it. Fear and Greed has held above 65 for seven consecutive days, a sustained stretch of confidence that suggests sellers are exhausted and buyers are in control. With zero high-impact USD data released this week, the path of least resistance remains up, but the silence is deceptive.

## The Setup

The macro calendar for the current week is effectively empty. No Non-Farm Payrolls, no CPI, no Fed speeches. This absence of friction allows price action to be driven purely by flow and sentiment rather than fundamental shocks. The result is a smooth, albeit fragile, rally.

Sentiment metrics confirm this momentum. The Fear & Greed Index moved from 65 to 73 over the last week, indicating a shift from cautious optimism to outright greed. This is not a chaotic spike; it is a steady climb. When sentiment stays in this range without a corresponding catalyst, it usually means leverage is being added slowly, and shorts are being squeezed out of the market.

However, the upcoming week brings a potential volatility spike. The **FOMC Meeting Minutes** are scheduled for release on **October 7th at 18:00 UTC**. These minutes will provide insight into the Federal Reserve's recent deliberations and the internal debate over interest rate policy. While the minutes are backward-looking, the market often reacts to the tone of the debate. If the minutes reveal a divided committee or a hawkish undercurrent, the current risk-on regime could flip instantly. Until then, the market is trading on inertia.

## BTC — directional bias, conviction, key levels to watch, what invalidates the call

Bitcoin is trading at **$86,109**, up nearly 1% in the last 24 hours. The bias is bullish, but conviction is moderate. The lack of news means there is no fundamental pressure pushing price higher, only the absence of pressure pushing it lower.

The key level to watch is **$85,000**. This zone has acted as support during recent pullbacks. A sustained close below this level would suggest that the recent rally was a liquidity grab and that bears are regaining control. Conversely, a break above **$87,500** would signal that the current momentum is strong enough to challenge previous highs.

The invalidation point for the bullish call is a sharp drop below **$84,000**. If BTC falls below this level, it would indicate that the current greed is fading and that sellers are stepping in ahead of the FOMC Minutes. In that scenario, the bias would shift to neutral or bearish.

## ETH — same structure as BTC

Ethereum is trading at **$2,716.59**, up 0.57% in the last 24 hours. The bias is also bullish, mirroring Bitcoin's trajectory. However, ETH has shown slightly less strength than BTC in recent days, suggesting that altcoins are lagging behind the market leader.

The key level to watch is **$2,650**. This zone has held as support in the short term. A break below this level would weaken the bullish case for ETH. On the upside, a move above **$2,800** would confirm that Ethereum is catching up to Bitcoin's momentum.

The invalidation point for ETH is a drop below **$2,600**. If ETH falls below this level, it would suggest that the altcoin market is underperforming and that capital is rotating out of ETH into safer assets like BTC or stablecoins.

## Calendar Risks

The primary risk for the coming week is the **FOMC Meeting Minutes** on **October 7th**. This event is the only high-impact scheduled item. Traders should expect heightened volatility around the release time. The minutes could reveal a hawkish tilt, which would likely pressure risk assets.

Secondary risks include any unexpected geopolitical developments or unexpected data leaks from major central banks. While the calendar is clear, the market is sensitive to any hint of policy change.
