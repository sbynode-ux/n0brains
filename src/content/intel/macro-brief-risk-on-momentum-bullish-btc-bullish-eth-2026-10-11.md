The Crypto Fear & Greed Index just hit **73** (Greed), the highest reading in seven days, signaling that retail sentiment is aggressively turning bullish as inflation data cools.

We are in a clear risk-on regime. The market is pricing in a soft landing, and traders are loading up longs before the week’s major inflation print. This is not a time for caution; it is a time for precision. The trend is up, but the crowd is getting heavy.

## The Setup — what released and what didn't, why it matters

There were no high-impact USD macro events released in the last **30 days** that shifted the regime. The market has been digesting recent data without a major shock, allowing technicals and sentiment to take the wheel.

That changes on **October 14**, when the **Core CPI m/m** is forecast at **0.2%** (prev **0.3%**) and **Core CPI y/y** at **2.5%** (prev **2.4%**). The consensus expects inflation to cool further. If the print comes in at or below forecast, it validates the "soft landing" narrative and fuels the current greed. If it spikes above, the narrative breaks.

The sentiment data confirms this positioning. For the past week, the index has climbed from **59** to **73**. This is a sustained move, not a flash. Traders are comfortable. Comfort is dangerous. When everyone agrees on the direction, liquidity dries up on the other side, making reversals violent.

## BTC — directional bias, conviction, key levels to watch, what invalidates the call

**Bitcoin (BTC)** is in a bullish bias. The rising Fear & Greed index suggests momentum is still with the longs. We expect **BTC** to test higher resistance levels as the week progresses, driven by the anticipation of a dovish CPI print.

Conviction is high, but not unanimous. The move from **59** to **73** indicates a shift from cautious optimism to active greed. This is a healthy trend, but it requires fuel. The fuel will be the CPI data.

Key levels to watch are the recent swing highs. If **BTC** holds above these levels into the **October 14** release, the path of least resistance is up. The invalidation level is a break below the 4-hour support that formed during the recent dip. If **BTC** loses that support, the greed turns to panic.

We are not betting on a reversal. We are betting on continuation until the data says otherwise. The trend is your friend until the bend at the end.

## ETH — same structure as BTC

**Ethereum (ETH)** is following **BTC** but with higher beta. As risk appetite increases, **ETH** tends to outperform **BTC** in percentage terms. The current sentiment supports this. We expect **ETH** to rally harder than **BTC** if the CPI print is benign.

The bias is bullish. The conviction is slightly lower than **BTC** because **ETH** has more internal variables (DeFi TVL, token unlocks, L2 activity) that can disrupt the trend. However, the macro tailwind is clear.

Key levels for **ETH** are the psychological resistance points and the previous weekly highs. If **ETH** breaks these with volume, it signals a strong altseason rotation. The invalidation level is a break below the 200-day moving average on the daily chart. If **ETH** falls below this, the bullish bias is void.

We are watching **ETH** closely. It is the canary in the coal mine for risk-on sentiment. If **ETH** stalls, **BTC** will likely follow.

## Calendar Risks — top 2-3 upcoming events that could flip the call

The week of **October 12–16** is packed with data. These are the events that could flip the bullish call:

1. **October 14: Core CPI m/y (Forecast 2.5%, Prev 2.4%)**
This is the headline. A hot print (>2.6%) will crush sentiment. A cool print (<2.5%) will fuel the fire. The market is priced for a cool print. Surprise is the only risk.

2. **October 15: Retail Sales m/m (Forecast 0.3%, Prev 1.2%)**
Consumer spending is the backbone of the soft landing narrative. A drop below **0.3%** suggests weakening demand, which could be interpreted as dovish (good for crypto) or recessionary (bad for risk assets). The market will decide.

3. **October 16: Fed Chairman Warsh Speaks**
Any hawkish tone from the Fed Chair will override the data. If he hints at delayed rate cuts, the risk-on rally will reverse instantly. We are listening for any mention of "inflation persistence" or "higher for longer."
