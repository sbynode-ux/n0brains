The Federal Reserve held rates steady at **4.00%** on September 16, but the market didn’t blink. Instead, Bitcoin surged **2.48%** and Ethereum gained **3.24%** in the 24 hours following the press conference, proving that the "higher for longer" narrative has finally lost its bite.

## The Setup

The **[Federal Funds Rate](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fedrates.htm)** decision was the only high-impact USD event released in the past week, and it confirmed what the futures market had already priced in: a pause. The **FOMC Statement** offered no new hawkish surprises, and the **FOMC Economic Projections** (SEP) failed to signal an accelerated cutting path, leaving the **4.00%** rate intact.

Usually, a "no news" statement triggers volatility as traders parse every word. This time, the silence was interpreted as stability. The absence of further tightening signals removed the primary overhang on risk assets. With the central bank effectively saying "enough," capital rotated back into speculative buckets. The **Fear & Greed Index** has climbed from a neutral **50** to a greedy **63** in just one week, suggesting that the fear of missing out is currently outweighing the fear of inflation.

We are now entering a week with **zero** scheduled high-impact USD events. This is a rare liquidity vacuum where price action is driven purely by technicals and cross-asset flows rather than macro data shocks. In these environments, direction tends to persist until a technical level breaks. The market is choosing to believe that the peak of the hiking cycle is behind us, even if the cuts haven't started yet.

## BTC

Bitcoin’s move above **$78,000** is significant because it shows resilience in the face of a hawkish hold. The **2.48%** daily gain is not an anomaly; it’s a continuation of the post-FOMC relief rally. The bias is **bullish** in the short term, driven by the lack of immediate downside catalysts.

Key levels to watch are the **$78,200** resistance zone. A clean break and close above this area opens the path to the **$80,000** psychological barrier. If Bitcoin fails to hold the **$76,500** support level, the bullish thesis weakens, suggesting the rally was merely a dead-cat bounce into a lower-high structure. However, the current momentum suggests buyers are defending this range aggressively.

The invalidation of this call is simple: a sudden spike in the DXY (US Dollar Index) triggered by unexpected geopolitical news or a shift in Treasury yields. Without a macro trigger to push the dollar higher, BTC has the runway to test higher liquidity pools.

## ETH

Ethereum is outperforming Bitcoin on a percentage basis, gaining **3.24%** to trade at **$2,512**. This relative strength indicates that capital is rotating into altcoins, a classic sign of late-cycle bull market behavior or, at minimum, a strong risk-on appetite. The **ETH/BTC** pair is holding its ground, which is crucial. If ETH were losing ground to BTC, it would signal that traders are fleeing to safety. Instead, they are leaning into alpha.

The bias for ETH is **bullish**, but with lower conviction than BTC because it relies on broader market liquidity. The key level is **$2,500**. A break below this level would suggest that the altcoin rally is exhausted. A hold above **$2,550** confirms the strength of the rotation. Traders should watch for volume spikes on ETH chains, as this often precedes larger moves in tokenized assets and DeFi protocols.

The risk here is a sudden BTC correction. ETH rarely decouples for long. If BTC stalls, ETH will likely bleed against the dollar, even if it holds its value against BTC. The current setup favors longs, but position sizing should account for the potential for a sharp, low-volume pullback.

## Calendar Risks

The week ahead is empty of high-impact USD data. This is not a lack of risk; it is a **hidden risk**. When the macro calendar is silent, price action becomes more sensitive to technical breaks and whale movements. A sudden liquidation cascade on Hyperliquid or a major exchange outage could drive price without a fundamental reason.

The primary risk is complacency. Traders may assume the path is smooth because there are no reports to miss. This is when traps are set. A break of a key support level on low volume can lead to a rapid, unfillable slide. The lack of news means there is no narrative to justify a reversal, so any move is likely to be technical and swift. We must remain vigilant for shifts in funding rates or open interest that might signal an impending squeeze.
