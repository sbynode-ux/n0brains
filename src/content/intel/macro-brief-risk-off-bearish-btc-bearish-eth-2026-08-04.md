The regime is risk-off: sentiment spent **7** straight days in Fear or worse, and Friday's **Non-Farm Employment Change** is the only print heavy enough to flip it. Everything else this week is noise.

## The Setup

Yesterday's **ISM Manufacturing PMI** hit the tape at **14:00 UTC** with a **54.0** forecast against **53.3** prior — a second reading in expansion. Crypto's response: nothing. The **Fear & Greed Index** sat at **28** on Saturday, **27** around the release, and **25** the morning after. When a scheduled macro event that moves the dollar barely moves crypto, the market isn't ignoring it — it's saving its powder for payrolls.

Friday's jobs report is the whole ballgame, and the baseline is fragile. Last month's Non-Farm Employment Change printed **57K**, a slowdown number that had the market repricing rate cuts. The forecast is **85K** — stabilization, not recovery. The spread between an 85K print and a **45K** print is the difference between a relief rally and a cascade. That's the trade.

## BTC

Directional bias: **Bearish**. Conviction: moderate — this is a positioning call, not a prediction. Spot sits at **$63,697**, up **1.63%** in the last **24 hours**, and that bounce is exactly the kind of low-trust rally that forms under Extreme Fear. The 25 print two days ago means leveraged longs already got purged once. This bounce is the market catching its breath, not changing its mind.

Key levels: support at **$62,000**, then **$60,000** — the line algos defend, and the last barrier before a slide toward **$58,000**. Resistance at **$65,500**, then **$66,500**. A daily close above $66,500 invalidates the bearish call; it would take a payrolls number hot enough to signal growth without killing the cut path. That's a narrow window.

What flips us bullish: an NFP headline below **60K** with the **Unemployment Rate** ticking to **4.3%**. Bad news becomes good news — the market front-runs the Fed's next cut and BTC can move **5–8%** in an hour. We're not fading that scenario. We're positioning for the base case until the data contradicts it.

## ETH

Directional bias: **Bearish**, with lower conviction than BTC. **ETH** at **$1,865.06**, up **1.15%** in the last day — but the bounce is thinner, and ETH's real problem isn't macro, it's the ratio. The market treats ETH as leveraged beta on BTC: it gains **0.6%** for every **1%** BTC gains, and it falls harder when the tape breaks. That's not a hedge. That's a liability in a risk-off regime.

Key levels: **$1,800** is the floor that has to hold. Below it sits **$1,750**, then **$1,680**, where the last flush bottomed. Resistance: **$1,950**, then **$2,000** — a reclaim of $2,000 is the first real evidence the bear thesis is wrong. Invalidation: a weekly close above $2,000, or ETH holding $1,800 through a BTC drawdown on Friday. That relative strength would flip us neutral-to-long the ratio.

The asymmetry cuts both ways. Funding has been pinned negative for weeks, meaning shorts are crowded — but so are the leveraged longs stacked below the range. A **$100** move clears one side. We want to be on the side the data pushes.

## Calendar Risks

One event, three ways to hurt you. The Non-Farm Employment Change headline carries the macro weight: 85K forecast, 57K prior. Anything above **130K** kills the dovish repricing and sends BTC toward $60,000. Anything at or below **40K** does the opposite — cuts get pulled forward and crypto rips.

The Unemployment Rate, forecast to hold at **4.2%**, is the quiet trap. A tick to 4.3% matters more than a headline miss, because it forces the Fed's hand into September. The **Average Hourly Earnings** print, also **0.3%** forecast against 0.3% prior, is the stagflation switch: **0.4%** means hot wages plus soft hiring — delayed cuts, questioned growth, the worst combo for crypto. **0.2%** is the best case.

All three land at **12:30 UTC** Friday into a thin August tape. Liquidity is low, algorithms are trigger-happy, and the first print to move the dollar will cascade into crypto within seconds.
