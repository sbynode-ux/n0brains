## The size was the story

A **$45 billion** AUM in under a year is unprecedented. It signals that institutional money was desperate for AI exposure and Aschenbrenner was the guy handing out the tickets. But scale doesn't fix leverage — it amplifies it. When your thesis is "compute is the new oil" and you lever that thesis 5x, a 20% drawdown in the underlying isn't a dip. It's a death spiral.

The fund's collapse wasn't a bug in the AI models he was trading. It was a bug in the risk engine. Position sizing that assumed volatility would stay quiet. It never does.

When the selling started, there were no bids. The liquidation cascade fed on itself — forced sellers hitting bids, pushing prices down, triggering more forced sellers.
