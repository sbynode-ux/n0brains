Strategy spent **$176.3 million** buying back its own stock last week. In the same period, it bought only **334 Bitcoin** for **$28.7 million** [[1]](https://cointelegraph.com/news/strategy-848k-bitcoin-strc-buybacks-outpace-btc-buys?utm_source=rss_feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=rss_partner_inbound). That is a nearly **6:1 ratio** of corporate treasury maneuvering to actual Bitcoin acquisition.

For years, the market priced in Strategy’s relentless BTC buying as a primary bid side. That bid is no longer growing at the same velocity. The capital allocation has shifted from net-new demand into equity management. This is not a bearish signal for Bitcoin itself, but it is a structural change in the order flow that traders ignore at their peril.

## The Capital Allocation Shift

Strategy’s CEO Michael Saylor has long argued that the company’s stock is undervalued relative to its Bitcoin holdings. Buying back shares is a way to increase earnings per share and consolidate ownership. But from a market microstructure perspective, it changes the nature of the demand.

Previously, every dollar spent by Strategy added a fixed amount of BTC to the circulating supply cap. Now, that dollar is absorbed by the equity market. It reduces the float, which can lift the stock price, but it does not directly reduce BTC supply or push the price higher through pure scarcity mechanics.

This is a divergence between corporate finance logic and crypto macro logic. The market now has to price two separate narratives: Strategy’s equity performance and Bitcoin’s spot demand. When they decouple, volatility in the stock can increase without corresponding moves in BTC.

## Metaplanet’s Counter-Strategy

While Strategy pivots to buybacks, [Metaplanet](https://cointelegraph.com/news/metaplanet-net-income-strategy-bitcoin-accumulation?utm_source=rss_feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=rss_partner_inbound) is doubling down on accumulation using a net income strategy [[2]](https://cointelegraph.com/news/metaplanet-net-income-strategy-bitcoin-accumulation?utm_source=rss_feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=rss_partner_inbound). Metaplanet revealed a revised capital allocation that prioritizes buying Bitcoin with its operational cash flow.

This highlights a growing bifurcation in institutional Bitcoin exposure. One faction treats BTC as a balance sheet asset to be leveraged against equity. The other treats it as the primary treasury reserve. Metaplanet’s approach is more directly bullish for spot price action because it creates consistent, recurring buy pressure regardless of stock sentiment.

Traders need to watch which model gains traction. If Strategy’s buybacks boost its stock price but fail to drive BTC accumulation, the premium on BTC may compress. If Metaplanet’s model scales, it adds a new layer of structural demand that is less sensitive to equity market cycles.

## The Macro Pulse

Bitcoin is currently fighting to reclaim its 2026 yearly open [[3]](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/btc-price-fights-to-reclaim-2026-open-three-things-to-know-in-bitcoin-this-week?utm_source=rss_feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=rss_partner_inbound). This technical battle is happening against a backdrop of shifting institutional flows. The removal of Strategy’s aggressive spot buying as a primary bid side means other sources of demand must fill the gap.

That could come from ETF inflows, corporate treasuries like Metaplanet, or retail FOMO. But it also means the price discovery process is more fragmented. Liquidity is thinner in key levels because one of the largest market makers has stepped back from direct spot accumulation.

This fragmentation increases the risk of sudden liquidations. When a large bid side withdraws, price can drop faster than expected before new buyers step in. Traders holding high leverage into this transition are exposed to volatile wicks that don’t reflect long-term fundamentals.

## Market Context
