## The three real edges

[Arkham's research on trading systems](https://info.arkm.com/research/trading-systems-automations-algorithms-copy-trading-and-crypto-trading-bots) breaks down how profitable traders actually operate. The buckets are simple: speed, sophistication, or data. Speed means latency arbitrage — being first to react to a whale move or an exchange imbalance. Sophistication means complex multi-leg strategies that most retail traders can't even name. Data-driven means using APIs to turn raw on-chain and market information into clean, executable signals.

Notice what's missing: intuition, vibes, and "I've got a good feeling about this coin." The traders who consistently make money have removed themselves from the decision loop. They've built systems that do the thinking for them. The most profitable traders aren't smarter. They're faster, more systematic, and brutally disciplined about letting data override emotion.

## Copy trading is a bridge — and a trap

Copy trading is what happens when retail traders want automation without building it themselves. It's the easiest way to inherit someone else's entries — and their risk, their drawdowns, and their worst days. A copied trade without the original trader's risk model is like borrowing a race car without brakes. You're going fast, but you're not in control.

Bots are the same story. A bot without a proper risk framework isn't an edge. It's just a faster way to lose. The ones that survive aren't cleverer — they're built on inputs that actually matter: positioning crowding, liquidation distance versus real volatility, and scheduled event risk inside the trade's horizon. That's the difference between a bot that prints and a bot that blows up.
