### The Scope Fallacy

An audit is a snapshot, not a continuous shield. It verifies that specific lines of code function as intended within a narrow set of assumptions. It does not verify the broader ecosystem.

The latest wave of exploits didn't come from smart contract bugs in the core lending logic. They came from the periphery. Oracle manipulations, bridge vulnerabilities, and governance front-running. These are "operational" risks, not "code" risks. Auditors rarely touch the oracle feeds or the external bridge integrations because they aren't part of the core contract.

This leaves a massive blind spot. A protocol can have a flawless, award-winning smart contract and still lose every cent of its liquidity because the oracle it trusts was spoofed. The code was safe. The data was poisoned. The money is gone.

### The Bridge Vulnerability

Bridges are the weakest link in the crypto supply chain. They connect isolated blockchains, creating complex trust assumptions. When hackers minted **trillions in fake Bitcoin** on the 15 BTC bridge earlier this week, the underlying Ethereum contracts weren't necessarily hacked. The bridge logic allowed the minting because the cross-chain message validation failed.

### Operational Risk Is Real Risk

The report highlights that August incidents added significant operational context. These weren't sophisticated nation-state attacks. They were opportunistic probes against known misconfigurations. Bots are actively probing exposed Bitcoin payment servers to steal master keys. Ledger users still can't send ATOM after the Cosmos outage. These are infrastructure failures, not code failures.
