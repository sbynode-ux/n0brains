The Coldcard hack has no definitive loss figure, and that uncertainty is the most damning detail of the whole incident. Investigators are stitching together victim reports and onchain analysis, and the estimates don't even agree with each other. In a market that moves on **$4B** stablecoin supply shifts, a hack where we can't even count the damage is a signal in itself.

## The forensic fog

[The Coldcard hack](https://cointelegraph.com/news/coldcard-losses-how-investigators-trace-stolen-bitcoin) is being investigated through a patchwork of self-reported losses and blockchain sleuthing. Some wallets are obvious — drained, dusted, abandoned. Others are tangled through mixers, cross-chain bridges, and privacy protocols. The result: no single authoritative number. That's not a failure of the investigators. It's a feature of Bitcoin's pseudonymous design. When the trail goes cold, the "loss" becomes a narrative, not a fact.

This matters beyond the hardware wallet crowd. If we can't quantify a theft in real time, we can't price its market impact. A **$50M** hack and a **$500M** hack move BTC differently. Right now, the market is trading on a guess.

## The exhaustion signal

Meanwhile, [CryptoQuant data](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/bitcoin-sell-pressure-closer-to-exhaustion-after-4b-usdt-market-cap-drop-cryptoquant) shows Bitcoin sell pressure is "closer to exhaustion" after a **$4B** drop in USDT market cap. That's a contraction in stablecoin liquidity — fewer dollars ready to buy the dip. Combined with the Coldcard uncertainty, you have a market that's fragile but not collapsing. The sellers are tired. The buyers are absent. That's a setup for a squeeze, not a crash.

But don't confuse exhaustion with reversal. A market can be exhausted and still chop sideways for weeks. What matters is the next catalyst, and that catalyst is likely macro. The Fed calendar doesn't care about your hardware wallet.
