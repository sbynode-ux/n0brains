Starknet is asking its community to pull the plug on Ethereum.

The vote centers on a single, existential question: should the largest zk-rollup in the ecosystem spin off as an independent Layer 1 to survive the coming quantum threat? The proposal isn't about better performance or lower fees. It is about cryptographic survival.

This is not a hypothetical scenario. It is a structural referendum on the future of trust-minimized scaling. For traders, builders, and anyone betting on the longevity of rollup liquidity, the outcome will dictate whether Ethereum remains the settlement layer for the next decade or becomes a legacy chain for assets that refuse to migrate.

## The Quantum Clock

The core argument for the exit is simple. Current Elliptic Curve Cryptography, which secures most blockchain signatures and state roots, is vulnerable to Shor’s algorithm running on sufficiently powerful quantum computers. The timeline for this threat is debated, but the exposure is real.

If Starknet remains on Ethereum, it inherits Ethereum’s cryptographic assumptions. If those assumptions break, Starknet’s proofs and balances are theoretically compromised. By becoming a standalone L1, Starknet can implement post-quantum cryptography (PQC) signatures at the protocol level. They can move faster than Ethereum’s conservative upgrade schedule allows.

The risk is fragmentation. Liquidity is the lifeblood of any trading infrastructure. If you leave the Ethereum mainnet, you leave the deepest pool of capital, the most robust MEV infrastructure, and the established user base. You are betting that your standalone chain will be secure enough and fast enough to attract liquidity back from the ecosystem you are abandoning.

## Liquidity vs. Security Trade-off

Most Layer 2s are built on the promise of inheriting Ethereum’s security. Starknet’s zk-proofs are verified on-chain, meaning the security guarantee is tied to the Ethereum validator set. Breaking this link means accepting a lower level of security, however marginally, in exchange for cryptographic agility.

The vote forces a choice between two types of risk.

The first is the quantum risk. It is a long-tail, low-probability, high-impact event. Most traders operate on timeframes where this threat is negligible.

The second is the execution risk. Migrating an ecosystem is notoriously difficult. Smart contracts must be rewritten or bridged. Wallets must update. DeFi protocols must rebuild their integrations. If the migration fails, or if it succeeds but leaves the chain isolated, the value of the STRK token could collapse simply due to loss of network effects.
