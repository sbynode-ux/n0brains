Bitcoin [dipped below $75,000](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/bitcoin-price-under-75k-crypto-markets-tech-stock?utm_source=rss_feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=rss_partner_inbound) while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq posted green. The two assets, which have spent months moving in lockstep, just divorced for the first time in weeks.

This divergence is not a glitch. It is a signal.

The crypto market is currently decoupling from US equity beta. When Bitcoin ignores a tech-stock rally, it tells you two things about liquidity: either institutional flows have shifted from risk-on equities to defensive crypto holdings, or the leverage inside the crypto ecosystem is too fragile to absorb the upside without a washout. Given the recent liquidations in altcoins and the compression in BTC volatility, the latter is more likely.

## The Liquidity Disconnect

For the past six months, Bitcoin has acted as a leveraged bet on US technology earnings. When NVDA and MSFT climb, BTC follows. When they stumble, BTC bleeds. That correlation coefficient was hovering near **0.85** until last week.

Now it is dropping.

The market is realizing that US macro liquidity is not as abundant as the Federal Reserve’s recent tone suggested. Tech stocks are rallying on earnings beats and buyback programs, not on fresh central bank money. Bitcoin, however, is being dragged down by on-chain dynamics that have nothing to do with the Nasdaq.

We are seeing a classic "sell the news" reaction on the crypto side, layered over a "buy the dip" reaction on the equity side. This creates a vacuum. Traders who were long both sides are now unwinding their crypto exposure to cover equity positions or simply taking profits as the correlation breaks.

## The $75,000 Psychological Wall

**$75,000** is not just a number. It is a liquidity zone where **$200M+** in stop-losses and limit orders sit clustered.

When price action ignores the broader market rally, it means sellers are aggressive at every tick up. They are not waiting for a better macro backdrop. They are exiting. This is typical behavior for a market that is transitioning from a trend-following regime to a mean-reversion regime.

The dip below **$75,000** suggests that the buyers at this level are not institutions accumulating for a Q4 rally. They are short-term traders scalping volatility. This is a dangerous foundation for a sustained move higher. Without broad equity market participation, Bitcoin will struggle to break resistance.

## Macro Pulse: The Real Driver

The Federal Reserve’s next move is the only variable that matters right now. [Russia has finalized crypto trading rules](https://cointelegraph.com/news/russia-finalizes-crypto-trading-rules-november-rollout?utm_source=rss_feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=rss_partner_inbound) for a November 17 rollout, allowing retail BTC and ETH trades through Moscow Exchange. This is a supply-side event that will increase liquidity in the spot market but does not change the demand side driven by US rates.

Meanwhile, [Strive has bought 15,124 Bitcoin](https://cointelegraph.com/news/strive-buys-bitcoin-october-top-btc-corporate-holders?utm_source=rss_feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=rss_partner_inbound) in October, closing in on top corporate holders. This is a bullish signal, but it is incremental. It is not enough to overpower the technical selling pressure at **$75,000**.

The market is waiting for clarity on US inflation data. Until then, Bitcoin is trapped in a range. The tech stock rebound is providing a floor, but not a ceiling.

Bitcoin is currently testing support at **$74,500** with declining volume. DeFi TVL remains flat, indicating no new speculative capital is entering the ecosystem. Sentiment is neutral-to-bearish, with funding rates hovering near zero across major exchanges. The lack of leverage means a sharp pump is possible, but so is a slow bleed.
