**$124 million**. That is the amount of stolen cryptocurrency from the recent Binance breach that has already been swapped for Bitcoin, according to on-chain investigators at [Elliptic](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-11-06-binance-hackers-swap-stolen-tokens-124-million-bitcoin-exchange-plans-withdrawal-reopenings-435930).

The rest of the loot is still sitting in limbo. **$2.85 billion** remains frozen across various wallets, locked by the sheer difficulty of moving that much volume without triggering every regulatory and exchange alarm bell in existence.

This is not just a crime story. It is a market structure story. The hackers are not trying to cash out into fiat immediately. They are trying to obscure the trail by converting volatile, traceable altcoins into the one asset that offers the most liquidity and privacy: Bitcoin.

### The Bitcoin Sink

When bad actors convert stolen tokens into Bitcoin, they are effectively dumping risk onto the broader market while securing their profits in the hardest money available. Elliptic notes that little of the stolen crypto has likely been fully cashed out yet. This means the market is watching a slow bleed, not a sudden crash.

The hackers are playing a long game. They are likely using mixers, decentralized exchanges, and cross-chain bridges to fragment the funds. But every time they swap for BTC, they add buy pressure to Bitcoin specifically, while draining liquidity from the altcoin ecosystem they stole from.

This creates a perverse incentive. The market might see a slight dip in certain altcoins as panic sets in, while Bitcoin absorbs the inflow. It is a transfer of wealth from the reckless to the patient, facilitated by criminal enterprise.

### Exchange Liability and Frozen Assets

The fact that **$2.85 billion** is frozen is significant. It means that either the exchanges holding these funds have flagged them, or the wallets themselves are blacklisted across multiple chains. This is a rare instance of on-chain surveillance actually working at scale.

However, it also highlights the fragility of centralized custodians. Binance is planning more withdrawal reopenings, signaling a return to normalcy, but the shadow of this hack lingers. Traders holding funds on exchanges during such events are exposed to counterparty risk that no amount of marketing can erase.

If you are holding significant value on a centralized exchange, you are not just betting on the asset. You are betting on the exchange's security infrastructure. When that infrastructure is breached, the on-chain record shows who moved what, when, and where.

### The Human Cost of Digital Theft

While the numbers are staggering, the human element is often overlooked. These are not just "tokens." They are savings, investments, and trust. When hackers convert these assets into Bitcoin, they are eroding trust in the entire ecosystem.

The [New York Times](https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-11-08-onchain-data-casts-doubt-on-insider-trading-theory-in-nyts-suspicious-election-betting-story-436034) recently reported on suspicious betting patterns related to the election, though on-chain data cast doubt on insider trading theories. This shows a broader trend: as crypto becomes more integrated with traditional finance, every move is scrutinized. The same tools used to detect election betting irregularities are used to track hack proceeds.

Transparency is the enemy of crime. But it is also the friend of accountability.

### Market Context

Bitcoin is currently navigating a complex macro environment, with the US administration moving closer to allowing crypto in 401(k) retirement plans. This institutional adoption is driving long-term demand, while events like the Binance hack create short-term volatility. DeFi TVL remains resilient, but centralized exchange holdings are under increased scrutiny.
