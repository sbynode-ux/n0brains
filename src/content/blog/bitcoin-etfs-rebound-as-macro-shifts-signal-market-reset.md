Spot Bitcoin ETFs pulled in **$134.4 million** in just the first two trading days of October. That number matters more than the headline hype because it marks a decisive reversal from the **$134 million** in outflows that hit on September 30.

The market didn’t just bounce. It reset.

The catalyst was a weaker-than-expected U.S. jobs report. The data cooled Fed rate-hike bets almost instantly. Capital moved from defensive cash positions back into risk assets. Bitcoin caught that flow first. This is not a miracle. It is a mechanical reaction to macro liquidity conditions.

When the Federal Reserve’s policy trajectory shifts, institutional capital follows. The ETF inflows confirm that the "Uptober" narrative is being driven by real balance sheet expansion, not just retail FOMO.

## The Macro Lever

The jobs report is the single most important variable in the current trading environment. Weak data means less likelihood of aggressive tightening. Less tightening means cheaper money. Cheaper money flows into hard assets.

Bitcoin is priced as a liquidity sponge. When liquidity tightens, BTC drops. When liquidity loosens, BTC rises. The **$134.4 million** inflow is proof that the institutional bid is returning because the macro backdrop improved.

This correlation is consistent. It is not random. Traders who ignore the macro calendar are trading blind. Most of the traders liquidated in late September failed to adjust their leverage when the jobs data came in hot. They held positions that were statistically vulnerable to a liquidity crunch.

## El Salvador’s Survival

While Wall Street rotates capital, geopolitics is stabilizing a key Bitcoin holder. El Salvador’s **$666 million** Bitcoin reserve survived an IMF review with waived penalties. The waiver means El Salvador avoids further financial pressure that could force a liquidation of its BTC holdings.

The IMF’s decision is significant. It removes the threat of a forced sell-off from a sovereign entity. [El Salvador’s Bitcoin reserve survives IMF review](https://cryptoslate.com/el-salvadors-666-million-bitcoin-reserve-survives-imf-review/) confirms that the country will not accumulate more, but it also confirms they will not dump.

Sovereign accumulation is a long-term bid. Sovereign liquidation is a short-term crash. By keeping the reserve intact, the IMF removes a major overhang on the market. This is a quiet bullish signal that rarely gets headline coverage but affects the supply/demand equation.
