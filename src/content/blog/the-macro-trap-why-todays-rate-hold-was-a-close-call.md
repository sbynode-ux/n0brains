## The Illusion of Dovishness

Most traders read the headline "Fed holds rates" and assume the path to lower borrowing costs is clear. The minutes dismantle that narrative. The two dissenters who voted for a hike argued that inflation remained too high and that the labor market was still too tight. Even the majority that voted to hold did so with significant hesitation.

This hesitation matters more than the decision itself. It signals that the Fed is not convinced the inflation fight is over. They are waiting for more data, not celebrating progress. For traders, this means any bounce in risk assets is likely to be met with skepticism, not conviction.

The **10–2** split is the key number here. It shows that nearly **20%** of the committee was ready to tighten. When that percentage rises, volatility spikes. The Fed is not in "easing mode." It is in "wait-and-see mode," and that uncertainty is priced into every asset class.
