Logic dictates that speculative assets should sell off. The market initially tried that trade. Bitcoin dropped early in the week, reacting to the yield spike.

But it didn't break. It consolidated.

This resilience signals that the primary driver of BTC price action right now is not just macro liquidity, but the internal mechanics of the leverage market. But the cost of being wrong on the downside is currently higher than the cost of holding.

## The Decoupling Myth

For years, the narrative was simple: Bitcoin is a tech stock proxy. When yields go up, Bitcoin goes down. That trade is dead.

We are now in a phase where crypto is pricing in its own idiosyncratic risks—ETF flows, regulatory clarity, and on-chain liquidation maps—far more aggressively than it is pricing in bond yields. This divergence creates dangerous traps. Retail traders see the bond yield spike and assume BTC is doomed, so they short. They miss the bounce. Institutional algos see the same yield spike and assume BTC is strong, so they buy the dip. They get the better end of the trade.
