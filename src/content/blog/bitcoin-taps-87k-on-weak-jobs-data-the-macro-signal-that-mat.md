Bitcoin briefly touched **$87,000** this week. It didn’t stay there.

The move happened because US nonfarm payrolls missed expectations, sending Treasury yields lower. The market interpreted weak labor data as a signal that the Federal Reserve has room to cut rates. Rate cuts are usually bullish for risk assets. Bitcoin is a risk asset. The logic is simple. The execution is where traders fail.

Weak jobs data creates a specific macro environment. It lowers the cost of capital. It weakens the dollar. It pushes money into alternatives like crypto. But it also signals economic slowing. If the slowdown becomes a recession, equities crash. Crypto often correlates with equities in the short term. The result is volatility. Traders who bought the breakout at $87K without checking the horizon got caught in the reversal.

## The Macro Pivot

The core driver here isn’t Bitcoin. It’s the US dollar and bond yields. When yields drop, the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets like Bitcoin falls. This is basic finance. But the nuance is in the timing.

The market is pricing in a softer labor market. This means the Fed is likely to pivot to easing sooner rather than later. For crypto traders, this is the green light for leverage. But it’s also a trap. If the data suggests a hard landing, the initial rally in Bitcoin can be followed by a sharp sell-off as liquidity dries up in broader markets.
