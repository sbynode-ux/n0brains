Bitcoin fell to a four-month low near **$62,000** this week as spot ETFs recorded their seventh consecutive day of outflows, shedding **$2.1 billion** in November alone. The selloff is not a panic exit. It is a recalibration.

Institutional investors are not abandoning the asset. They are adjusting their leverage and timing. The market cap of the entire crypto sector dropped **4%** to **$2.26 trillion** in 24 hours, but the real story is in the flow data. For months, the narrative was "eternal bull market" driven by ETF inflows. Now, that narrative is breaking under the weight of macro reality.

## The outflow streak is a feature, not a bug

The **$185.4 million** lost on Friday alone might look scary to retail traders, but it is a drop in the bucket for the **$2.1 billion** November total. What matters is the consistency. Seven days of outflows suggests a coordinated shift in positioning by large players. They are taking profits or hedging against upcoming volatility.

This is not the same as the 2022 crash. Back then, outflows were driven by counterparty risk and liquidity crises. Today, the infrastructure is robust. The outflows reflect a strategic pause. Traders are wary of entering new long positions at all-time highs when the macro backdrop is shifting.

## Macro pressure is the real driver

The Bitcoin ETF outflows are happening in tandem with a broader risk-off sentiment in traditional finance. When the S&P 500 stalls, crypto rarely decouples for long. The correlation between tokenized equities and Bitcoin is tightening. Investors are moving cash into safer havens or staying on the sidelines until the Federal Reserve clarifies its next move.
