This isn’t a glitch. It’s the structural reality of low-liquidity hours. When institutional flows dry up and retail traders are asleep, the price discovery mechanism breaks. The asset doesn’t just move randomly; it moves inefficiently. And inefficiency is where the money is made—or lost.

Most traders ignore the calendar. They see a dip on Saturday and think "buy." They don’t see the depth chart. ## The Anatomy of the Weekend Discount

The data from the [Kraken Canadian dollar market analysis](https://cointelegraph.com/news/how-market-depth-shapes-the-price-of-a-weekend-bitcoin-sale?utm_source=rss_feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=rss_partner_inbound) reveals a brutal truth about market microstructure. Over a 14-hour window, the price divergence from the global average wasn’t a blip. It was a sustained, measurable dislocation. 

When you trade on weekends, you are not trading Bitcoin. You are trading the imbalance between supply and demand in a vacuum. There are no HFTs arbitraging the spread. There are no institutional desk traders rebalancing portfolios. There are just retail hands and the few market makers left on shift.

Market makers widen their spreads not because they are greedy, but because they are protecting themselves against the risk of being the last person holding the bag when Monday morning opens and the real price arrives. If you buy into that weekend dip, you are paying for the privilege of providing liquidity to someone who knows they can sell to you at a premium on Monday.
