Metaplanet is issuing **$329 million** in zero-interest convertible bonds to fund more Bitcoin purchases.

This is not a conservative treasury strategy. It is a leveraged bet that relies entirely on the token’s price appreciation to avoid dilution. The bonds mature in five years. If Bitcoin does not rise significantly above the conversion price, shareholders get diluted. If Bitcoin crashes, the company still owes the principal.

It is a binary outcome with asymmetric risk for equity holders.

## The Mechanics of the Trap

The core of this deal is the convertible bond structure. Investors lend Metaplanet money at **0% interest**. In exchange, they have the option to convert that debt into equity if the stock price rises.

For Metaplanet, this is cheap capital. But it is dangerous capital if the underlying asset behaves erratically. The company is using this debt to buy Bitcoin, which is already a volatile asset. They are effectively adding leverage to leverage.

[Metaplanet](https://cointelegraph.com/news/metaplanet-329m-convertible-bonds-pay-debt-share-buybacks) plans to use the proceeds to pay down existing debt and fund share buybacks. This keeps the equity price artificially supported while they load up on BTC exposure. It is a circular economy of confidence.

If the market turns, the buybacks stop. The debt remains. The Bitcoin position is underwater. The convertible bondholders then have the choice: take the loss on the debt or convert to equity and dilute everyone else.

## Bitcoin as a Liability, Not Just an Asset

Most corporate Bitcoin treasuries treat BTC as a store of value. Metaplanet is treating it as a high-beta equity proxy.

When you finance a volatile asset with fixed-term debt, you introduce a liquidity risk that doesn't exist in long-term holding strategies. If the price of Bitcoin drops by **20%** over the next year, Metaplanet still has to repay the bond principal unless they can roll the debt or convert it.

This creates a forced selling pressure scenario. If the company needs to raise cash to service other obligations or maintain liquidity, they may be forced to sell Bitcoin at the worst possible time. This is the opposite of "HODLing." It is "HODLing with a margin call."

## The Macro Disconnect

This move happens while global macro conditions remain uncertain. Interest rates are sticky, and risk assets are sensitive to liquidity shifts. A zero-interest bond is only attractive when you believe the asset you’re buying will outperform the cost of capital.

In a low-rate environment, this makes sense. In a high-volatility environment, it is a gamble.

The bond issuance also signals that Metaplanet believes its stock is undervalued. If they thought the stock was fairly priced, they would issue equity. If they thought it was overvalued, they would issue straight debt. Convertibles are a middle ground that assumes the stock will go up. That is a bullish signal, but it is also a signal of desperation for cheap capital.

## The Signal

This is a classic leverage cycle setup. Companies like Metaplanet are adding debt to buy volatile assets, creating a fragile ecosystem where a small downturn triggers a cascade of forced sales.

For traders, this is a signal to watch for increased volatility. When leveraged corporate treasuries are underwater, they become sellers. When they are profitable, they become holders. The risk is that the market moves against them faster than they can adjust.
