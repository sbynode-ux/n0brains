**Base** has completed its Cobalt upgrade, and the line between traditional finance and on-chain trading is blurring faster than most traders realize. The update isn't just another protocol patch; it introduces native transaction conditions and compliance checks directly into the settlement layer [Base](https://cointelegraph.com/news/base-cobalt-upgrade-tokenized-finance-b20-kyc).

This is the infrastructure that allows tokenized stocks to behave like stocks. Before Cobalt, tokenized equities were often just speculative derivatives with opaque risk profiles. Now, issuers can embed logic for stock splits, dividend distributions, and KYC gates at the protocol level. This changes the game for anyone trading tokenized assets on Hyperliquid or similar venues.

## The Compliance Layer

For years, the biggest friction point in tokenized equities was regulatory friction. Institutions couldn't touch them because they couldn't guarantee the buyer was accredited or that the token wasn't being traded by a sanctioned entity.

Cobalt solves this by giving issuers tools to enforce these checks on-chain. This doesn't mean every retail trader is locked out, but it means the liquidity pools for these assets are cleaner. The risk of a sudden regulatory takedown or a liquidity crunch caused by a compliance violation drops significantly.

For traders, this means tokenized assets like NVDA or AAPL on Base are becoming more reliable proxies for the underlying equity. The spread tightens, the depth increases, and the correlation to the real-world asset strengthens.

## Automated Corporate Actions

Stock splits and dividends used to be messy off-chain events that on-chain traders had to manually account for. If you held a tokenized share and a split occurred, your balance didn't automatically adjust unless the issuer manually updated the contract.

Cobalt lets issuers set transaction conditions that handle this automatically. When a split happens, the token supply and price adjust on-chain in real time. This eliminates a major class of operational risk. You no longer have to worry about holding the wrong version of a token or missing a corporate action because the data feed lagged.

This automation is critical for algorithmic traders. If your strategy relies on precise price feeds and token balances, manual reconciliation is a liability. Cobalt removes that liability.

## The Hyperliquid Connection

This upgrade is particularly relevant for traders on [Hyperliquid](https://cointelegraph.com/news/base-cobalt-upgrade-tokenized-finance-b20-kyc). Hyperliquid already supports tokenized equities, but the risk profile has shifted. With Base handling the compliance and distribution logic, the margin on Hyperliquid is now backed by a more robust settlement layer.

However, this also introduces new complexity. When tokenized stocks are leveraged on Hyperliquid, liquidations happen just like any other crypto asset. The difference is that the underlying asset is a regulated equity. A sudden move in the Nasdaq can trigger on-chain liquidations in milliseconds.

This is where traditional risk management fails. Traders often look at the price of the tokenized stock and ignore the real-world market hours, holidays, or pre-market volatility. Tokenized assets trade 24/7, but the underlying equity does not. This mismatch is a trap.
